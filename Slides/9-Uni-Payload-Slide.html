THE OBJECTIVE

Commercial UAV telemetry payloads suffer from high capital costs, vendor lock-in, and poor hardware modularity. External payloads create aerodynamic drag and draw directly from main flight batteries, inducing voltage instability and reducing flight endurance. Within a university engineering team developing an open-source, edge-AI wildfire-tracking drone platform, the assigned role required leading the structural engineering of a universal payload enclosure and designing an independent power system with zero electrical draw on the host platform.

THE EXECUTION

Engineered a modular, multi-tier vertical compute stack in SolidWorks housing a Raspberry Pi 5 and ESP32 microcontroller, incorporating thermal isolation to shield ambient temperature and humidity sensors from processor heat dissipation. Conducted FEA stress analysis in SolidWorks under high-vibration and operational loading conditions, optimizing structural geometry to minimize mass while maintaining mechanical integrity. Designed and prototyped a dedicated, isolated power distribution network using dual buck converters, delivering independent regulated power to the compute stack, servos, and sensor suite without interfacing with the host drone battery.

THE IMPACT

The CAD architecture and power network were manufactured using additive techniques and integrated into an operational telemetry platform. The physical system demonstrated tool-free sensor hot-swapping, low-latency edge-AI fire detection, and zero degradation of baseline host UAV flight endurance.
